**Design ([docs/design/r16-compositor.md](../../../docs/design/r16-compositor.md) §3.11-3.12, §7, §8.1; approved 2026-10-24):**
- A retained frame target per surface, plus a new `renderer_surface_draw_scene_damage` entry.
- Damage rects are merged and capped at **16 regions**, zgui's rule (`MAX_REGIONS`, zgui-gpu `lib.rs:6806-6916`), with a full redraw on a new surface or a resize. There is no percentage-of-window threshold.
- Damage is copied from the retained target to the back buffer, then presented with `Present1` dirty rects. In DirectComposition mode, partial present is used only if the readback test passes; otherwise it presents full.
- Damage expands by 3σ across blur groups, and blur passes are limited to the damaged region.
- The state-bind tracker and the path tessellation cache are independent and can land first.

- [ ] Native tests show unchanged pixels outside damage and correct present regions.
- [ ] A full-damage draw matches the legacy path pixel for pixel; a damaged redraw matches a full redraw bit-exactly over a scripted sequence.
- [ ] `Present1` is verified in both surface modes, and the full-present fallback fires when it fails.
- [ ] Integration branch only; green at ticket 54.

Every behaviour check is a browser-parity harness fixture (an HTML page recorded in headless Chrome plus the equivalent gpui-go view), unless stated otherwise.

## Answer

Landed on `native/r16` (scene service **v9** / renderer service **v6**, native revision 151 — 54 sets 16), in two commits (233ae0c, 72f382d) plus this answer.

**What landed**

- **The path tessellation LRU cache (§3.12, the independent half, commit 233ae0c):** a process-global 128-entry LRU in the scene service, keyed on the exact command+pool bytes plus the style's fill tolerance (the tolerance is part of the STYLE command's bytes already; hashing it again makes the key's independence explicit). A repeated `path_script` returns its cached vertices without re-running lyon — bit-exact by construction (the same bytes produced them); over the cap the least recently used entry evicts. Scene v9: the stateless `scene_tess_cache_stats` entry (35 pointers, 384 bytes; no records changed) reports the cumulative hits/misses/evictions and the entry count. (Note the Go client's two-call capacity protocol: one `PathBuilder.Build` is one miss — the capacity probe tessellates and caches — followed by one hit.)
- **The state-bind tracker (§3.12, same commit):** `draw_with_variant` and `dx_blur_pass` consult a per-frame `BindTracker` (Rc inside `FrameBindings`) caching the last-bound topology, viewport, VS/PS, the b0/b1 cbuffer pair, the DATA-register cbuffer (register- and stage-checked: the instance pipelines bind it at the vertex stage, the blur passes at both), the b5/b6 extension cbuffers, the primary sampler and the primary/instance-data SRV slots — the primary texture's VS and PS slots tracked SEPARATELY (the blur passes bind the pixel stage only, so a shared slot would let a later vertex bind ride on a pixel one). Only what changed binds; every raw bind of a tracked slot elsewhere in the walk (pre_draw, the group-begin viewport, the coverage restore, draw_surfaces' raw pipeline) invalidates the tracker. Zero pixel impact; the counter (`state_bind_count`) is cumulative and device-global, like the tracker (the shared immediate context keeps one pipeline state across surfaces and frames).
- **The retained frame target per surface (§3.11, commit 72f382d):** the offscreen scene texture persists across frames and always holds the last presented frame. The validity is tracked per surface — invalidated by a resize, a device rebuild, a clear-present, or a legacy draw that rendered directly into the swap chain — so a new/resized/recovered surface forces the documented full redraw.
- **`renderer_surface_draw_scene_damage` (renderer v6, slot 19):** the rects are clamped to the viewport, EXPANDED across every blur/drop-shadow group, mask, transform and backdrop whose region they intersect (viz's `ExpandDamageForPixelMovingFilters` rule, `direct_renderer.cc:964-993`/`1041-1042`; the blur dilation is the pin's composite dilation 3×radius — `filter_operation.cc:333-341`'s `sigma*3` shape; drop-shadow ops use their shadow quad; masks/transforms their group footprint), MERGED (nearby rects — the 8-px merge gap; a merged region only ever covers more) and CAPPED at 16 regions (zgui's `MAX_REGIONS`; over the cap — or an empty clamp result — redraws fully). NO percentage-of-window threshold. The redraw is SCISSORED to the damage union into the retained target (a scissor-enabled rasterizer state; the clear is a scissored constant pass — a 1×1 texture through the downsample pipeline, both clear colors exact under a 1×1 bilinear sample and premultiplication-invariant). Groups whose region intersects the damage box render their buckets UNSCISzerED: the blur reads the bucket beyond the composite quad (the kernel reach) and the transform composite's bilinear support reads past the quad's edge, so a partially-rendered bucket would diverge from a full redraw there (the conservative rule the design's risk 2 asks for). Each damage rect is then copied into the back buffer (`CopySubresourceRegion` — DXGI requires the app to have written every pixel of each dirty rect; the retained target makes the copy independent of buffer age — Chromium's own Windows pattern, `software_output_device_win_swapchain.cc:241-273`) and presented with `Present1` dirty rects. A Present1 failure falls back to a full present for the frame.
- **The DComp readback test (the design's Q5):** on the first partial present of a mode-0 surface the entry reads back the next back buffer and compares it with the retained target; a mismatch disables partial presents for that surface permanently (every later frame presents full — the damage-scissored redraw still saves the GPU work). **On this machine the DComp verification FAILS and the fallback fires (partial=0, full=1) while the hwnd mode ACCEPTS Present1 (partial=1)** — both outcomes are pinned by `TestR53Present1InBothSurfaceModes`, and the DComp result matches Chromium's own observation that the GPU DComp path ignores update rects (`skia_output_device_dcomp.cc:213-219`).
- **`renderer_surface_read_frame_pixels` (slot 20):** the staging readback of the RETAINED FRAME TARGET — the test seam for the equivalence gates (the back buffer rotates with the flip chain; the retained target does not).
- **The extended debug stats (36 bytes, append-only after `record_size`):** `state_bind_count` (the tracker, device-global), `tess_cache_hits` (the scene service's global counter), `damage_fraction` (the last frame's merged damage area over the window area, per-mille — 1000 = full) and the cumulative `full_present_count`/`partial_present_count`. Two new table scalars: `size_of_damage_rect`, `max_damage_regions`.
- **The region-limited blur + 51's handed-off bounds-sized FILTER buckets:** the downsample/gaussian passes shade only the composite region plus the kernel reach (the two separable gaussians read ~3σ per axis past their position and the second reads the first, so the downsample region carries twice the reach plus a few texels of bilinear support), reading the full source — the in-region values are bit-identical to the full-window chain. The passes limit through the **SCISSOR, not a viewport offset**: the fullscreen blur passes sample through viewport-relative texture coordinates, so an offset viewport samples the wrong texels — found by the r52 drop-shadow fixture going white, fixed by re-running the region math as a scissor rect (`dx_blur_pass_region`). The filter buckets carry the chain's read margin (the content extent, the record bounds dilated by 9×radius — the composite's 3σ plus the chain's read reach — and each drop-shadow op's shadow quad dilated by its own reach), quantized to the 256-px granularity and clamped to the window: a region reaching the window edge keeps R15's window-sized bucket AND its window blur pair, so the R15 blur fixtures stay bit-exact **by construction** (re-verified: the parity filters/shadow/gradient/boxpaint packages all green).
- **The Go half:** the renderer v6 mirror (the table, `DamageRect`, the extended `DebugStatsRecord`, `DrawSceneDamage`, `ReadFramePixels`), the scene v9 mirror (`TessCacheStats`), the gpui `Surface.DrawSceneDamage`/`Surface.ReadFramePixels` seam (the same atlas/recovery guards as `DrawScene`) — the test seam the ticket asked for; NOT the Go damage computation (58), paint flashing (58) or the frame-loop fast path (60).

**Deviations from the design**

1. **The blur region limiting uses the SCISSOR, not the bound viewport** (§3.11's "binds their viewport to the dilated composite region"): the fullscreen blur passes (downsample/gaussian) sample through viewport-relative texture coordinates, so a viewport OFFSET makes them sample the wrong texels — the r52 blurred-drop-shadow fixture caught it (the shadow vanished). The scissor keeps the sampling math identical to the full-window chain and only bounds the shaded fragments, which is the actual cost cut (the gaussian taps are the fragment work). The composite/downsample position-based math is unaffected either way.
2. **The merge gap is 8px** — zgui's "merge nearby rects" rule was relayed through the design (§8.1 answer 3) because the zgui-head checkout was absent under `%TEMP%\research-src\`; the gap constant is the local choice, documented in `RENDERER_ABI.md`. The 16-region cap and the no-percentage-threshold rule (the parts the ticket names) are implemented and gated as specified.
3. **The damage walk needs no per-requirements "not damage-aware" flag** (risk 2's mitigation sketch): the walk is conservative everywhere — the scissor bounds every draw (batches, paths, surfaces, composites, the coverage rasterization, the blend backdrop copy), the expansion covers every pixel-moving group/backdrop, and groups intersecting the damage box render their buckets fully — so no plan feature can draw outside the damage or read partially-rendered content. The equivalence gate over the scripted sequence (including the blur group straddling the edge) is the proof the design asked for.
4. **The DComp partial present effectively does not engage on this machine** (the readback test fails, so mode 0 presents full after one probe) — exactly the design's Q5 fallback. The hwnd mode (Chromium's own software path) accepts Present1; both behaviors are pinned by the both-modes test.
5. The state-bind counter is device-global (not per-surface): the tracker mirrors the shared immediate context's single pipeline state, so a per-surface split would be a fiction; the debug-stats record documents it, and the test brackets its own draws for the delta.
